## 3. Where our information comes from
We compile information from public sources: court dockets, government and regulatory databases (such as SEC EDGAR and IAPD, and the OFAC and other government sanctions lists), company registries, and published press. Where clearly marked, we also show unconfirmed reports from public sources (section 5) and information submitted by users (section 6).

Each entry shows its source and the date we last checked it. We report what those sources say. We do not independently confirm every underlying allegation, and an entry reflects its source as of the date shown. Records can be incomplete, out of date or wrong.

## 4. No findings of wrongdoing
A lawsuit, complaint, regulatory filing, news article, report or list entry is not proof that anyone did anything wrong. Allegations in lawsuits are only allegations; many are dismissed or settled with no admission. We describe court and government records as what they are (for example, "according to a complaint filed in [court] on [date]") and show the outcome when we know it.

Labels such as "flag" or "check" describe the type of public record under our published methodology (see "How we check"). They are not statements about anyone's character or conduct, and they are not recommendations to take or refuse an investment.

## 5. Unconfirmed reports
Some entries are reports found publicly online that are not official records, for example press stories, published interviews, or public posts. We show an unconfirmed report only when:

- it links to a **named public source** that anyone can check;
- it is **attributed** to that source ("According to [source], …"), not stated by us as fact;
- it is **clearly labeled "Not confirmed by official records"**; and
- it is dated.

We do not publish anonymous gossip, rumors without a public source, or private information. Showing a report means only that the source said it. It does not mean we believe or endorse it. The firm or person named can reply (section 9), and we remove any item shown to be false.

## 6. Sanctions and watchlist data
Sanctions entries come from official government lists (such as the U.S. Treasury OFAC Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) List, the EU Consolidated List and the UK sanctions list) and from services that collect those lists. We show each entry with its official source and listing date.

- A **confirmed match** means the official listing identifies that person or entity.
- A **possible name match** means a name (and any identifiers shown) looks like a listed party. It does **not** mean they are the listed party.
- **Nationality, citizenship, residence or place of business is never, on its own, a sign of sanctions status or wrongdoing**, and we don't present it as one.

Always check the official list before you rely on a sanctions entry.

- accuse anyone of a crime, fraud or sanctions violation unless an official record or named public source says so (cite it);
- state as fact something you know is false or misleading, or present rumor as fact;

- disclose information you are bound by contract or law to keep confidential; or
- be submitted by someone with a conflict of interest (for example a competitor, or a current employee of the firm) without saying so.

We may decide in our discretion whether a submission meets these standards. Reviewing, editing for accuracy, labeling, declining or removing submissions is an editorial choice that does not make us the author of user-provided information.
